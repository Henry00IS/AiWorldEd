# Column sections and profile presets

## Sections, rotation and twist

- Depth / width is a generator-wide aspect ratio; Round becomes an oval.
- Select one segment or marquee-select points, then use Section to apply a type to every joining segment. S cycles Round, Square, Polygon, Cross, Bar and Stadium. Graph bands are blue for square, gold for polygon, orange for cross, pink for bar and light blue for stadium, teal when the corners are cut or rounded; dashed bands indicate twist.
- Square and Polygon have **Corner** and **Corner Sides**. Corner is how far each cut reaches along the two sides, as a fraction of half a side: 0 keeps sharp corners, 1 meets at the side middles. Corner Sides is the number of faces across each corner, 1 to 8: 1 is a straight chamfer, more faces follow the bevel modifier's round profile, a circle arc touching both sides. A square with corner 1 and many sides approaches Round. Corner values stay when switching between Square and Polygon; Round drops them.
- A chamfer reaching 2 − √2 ≈ 0.59 of half a side makes a regular octagon (before aspect scaling). Aspect scales the corners too, so rectangle fillets are elliptical rather than independently circular.
- **Cross** is a plus made of two crossed bars, like a syringe plunger or a Phillips tip. **Arm Width** is each arm's thickness as a fraction of the section width (0.05 to 0.95). Both arms keep the same thickness when Depth / width stretches the cross. It builds five convex brushes per run, a centre and four arms ending flush on its sides, so every outer face lies on one outline edge and the CSG result has no inner faces. Tapering a cross to zero radius ends every piece in one apex.
- **Bar** is a single flat bar along X, the one-armed counterpart of the cross, for blades, paddles and crossguards. **Thickness** is a fraction of the section width and ignores Depth / width like the cross. A profile scales width and thickness together. **End** is the thickness at the top of the segment: the bar tapers linearly from Thickness to End along the segment, a smooth wedge with sloped faces, down to 0 for a sharp edge (a chisel or screwdriver tip). Inserting a point splits the taper at the new point, deleting one joins it, and Mirror swaps the two ends. A preset range with an End spreads one taper over all its segments. Bars without End are built as before.
- **Stadium** is a slot with two flat sides along X and half-circle ends. **Straight** is the flat side length as a fraction of the width; the depth is the end diameter, the rest of the width. Each end uses half the column **Sides**, and each flat side stays one flat face per piece, shaded hard while the ends shade smooth.
- Polygon supports 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 and 12 faces. **Sides** controls Round sections only. Each Square or Polygon section uses its own natural outline: one point per sharp corner, or one point per corner face plus one per side when cut. Redundant collinear points are removed from the final brush. Changing one section's corner settings never changes neighbouring Round sections. Each brush uses one section for both rings; section changes split convex runs at their shared horizontal plane, without requiring matching ring counts.
- Twist is degrees over each segment, accumulated from the bottom, bounded to ±1440 degrees. Sampling adds sub-rings at no more than 5 degrees per band. Inserting a point divides the original twist, deletion adds neighboring twists, and Mirror reverses handedness.
- **Rotation** turns a whole segment rigidly about the column axis, in degrees (±360), like a block on a pole. It is an absolute offset for that segment only: the segment starts at the twist accumulated below plus its own rotation, its own Twist turns on top of that, and the next segment starts from the accumulated twist again without inheriting the rotation. Where neighbouring segments differ in rotation, the joint gets two rings at the same height, one per orientation, and the runs split there; equally rotated untwisted neighbours still merge into one convex brush. Rotation applies to every section type, Round included (its facets and oval aspect turn), and side UVs turn with the section. Rotated bands use a solid band in the graph even when Round. Inserting a point gives both halves the rotation; deleting keeps the lower segment's; Mirror keeps rotations. Defaults are 0, stored as an optional eighth segment value, so older curves and presets read unchanged.
- Twisted bands use small **convex hull envelopes** of successive rotated rings. The planar triangular boundary is a controlled approximation of a swept twist, not an exact ruled/helical surface. Changing rotation prevents run merging. Profile 1.0 uses the unrotated box half-width (limited by depth / aspect), independent of section type or twist. Rotation never rescales a section or its neighbors: a square that fills the box before rotation may have corners outside the box after rotation. Polygon sections keep their existing radius convention. Untwisted Round sections retain their original ring orientation, rather than inheriting an earlier twist that would visibly rotate their faceted outline; accumulated rotation is retained for Square, Polygon and actively twisted sections.
- Square and polygon sides and chamfer faces are flat by default. Their twisted versions use separate smoothing classes for each intrinsic face ribbon: smooth along the twist, hard across true corners. Round and rounded corners (two or more corner sides) use the existing max angle. Caps and untwisted flat sides remain flat. Bake and scene saves preserve smoothing classes.

## Blend between sections

- A segment with **Blend** on morphs from a neighbour whose outline differs instead of stepping at their shared point: a square nail point fades into the round shank, a screwdriver blade flares out of its shaft, a whiskey bottle's square shoulder rounds into the neck. The checkbox sits next to Section and appears only for segments that touch such a change. It applies to the selected segments on the main line and is one undo step.
- A point is a blendable change when both segments rise along the profile, the point has a width, and the outlines differ: section type, polygon sides, corner settings, bar or cross thickness (a tapered bar's End counts at its top), stadium straight, side mode, fixed depth or rotation. Twist does not count. A vertical step in the profile is a step on purpose and never blends.
- The transition is the whole blending segment; there is no length setting. Insert a point to make it shorter. At each blending end the outline goes from the neighbour's ring at the shared point to the segment's own ring at its other end; where both segments at a point blend, they meet at a 50/50 mix. Between, each ring is the weighted Minkowski sum of the two outlines, scaled to the profile, so the shape stays inside the box and never bulges past either outline. A long blending segment fades its own section away over its length, so Blend suits short collars, tips and throats.
- Each span between rings builds as one convex hull, exact for straight segments and a fine approximation along curves. A **Cross** is split into its two bars, each morphed on its own, and each span is cut along the diagonals into four convex wedges, one per arm, so a Phillips tip becomes a fluted cone without inner faces. Bar End tapers, the side modes and fixed depth morph with their outlines. Blended faces use angular U and arc-length V like twisted bands; long level edges stay flat and the rest shades smooth.
- Changing a segment's section turns Blend on at each new change point for the shorter of the two segments (the changed one on a tie), unless one of them blends already. Untick it to keep a hard step.
- Blend is stored as an optional segment value. Curves without it, including every scene and preset saved before it, build exactly the same brushes as before. Graph bands of blending segments fade from the neighbour's colour into their own.
- Presets that blend: Nail (the ground point's corners sit on the shank circle), Tent Peg (cross arms flare into the round cap), Flathead and Phillips Screwdrivers (blade and cross tip out of the shaft), Whiskey Bottle (shoulder), Canoe Paddle (throat) and Antenna (base cone and tip cap). Turnery shoulders, architectural blocks and twisted columns keep their hard steps.

## Sides, fixed depth and the secondary line

- Bar, Square and Stadium segments have a **Side** mode: **Mirror** (both sides follow the profile, as before), **−X only** and **+X only** (one side follows the profile while the other stays at the narrowest value of the run, a flat back), and **Custom** (the profile shapes +X and a second line shapes −X). Round, Polygon and Cross always mirror.
- Square and Stadium segments have **Fixed depth**: the depth stays at that of the run's lowest ring with width, while the width follows the profile. A pier that flares into a slab keeps flat front and back faces; a gable keeps its depth up to the ridge. A Stadium keeps half-circle ends of that depth and lengthens its flat sides.
- Sided pieces are built from horizontal slices, each one convex brush; a custom piece is also sliced at every point of the secondary line.
- When a segment uses Custom, the curve editor shows **Line: Main | −X side** in the toolbar, and **Tab** switches between them. The line being edited is drawn at full strength and is the only one whose points can be picked; the other is faded behind it (main orange, secondary teal). The secondary line has no sections of its own, so the section controls hide while it is edited. **Mirror** mirrors both lines; other tools act on the edited line. The secondary line is stored inside the profile, so it syncs, undoes and saves with it, and curves without one are stored exactly as before.
- Presets in **Piers & Buttresses**, **Walls, Beams & Trim** and **Brackets & Corbels** use these modes: the T-shaped **Flared Pier**, the one-sided **Half-Arch Springer**, **Stepped Buttress**, **Stepped Corbel**, **Console Bracket**, **Shelf Bracket**, **Wall Pilaster**, **Chimney Breast**, **Stepped Retaining Wall**, **Gable Wall**, **Merlon**, **Keystone**, **Chamfered Plinth** and **Haunched Beam** (turn it 90° to lay it down). The **Knife** has a clip-point blade and the **Chef's Knife** a curved edge with a straight spine, both drawn with a custom −X line.

## Curve editor presets, markers and grid

- The preset picker starts with **Reset to 1.0**, **Reset to 0.5** and **Reset to 0.0** in their own _New Curve_ group: flat curves that start a new profile. Every curve editor offers them, whatever its field.
- Every preset has a plain object name and a tooltip, shown when hovering its entry and on the closed picker, with a sentence on where the shape comes from or is found and a level-design tip.
- **Base** and **Top** remember their place while switched off (the toolbar toggles and right-click on a marker switch them off), and switching one on restores it. Every preset stores its own base and top places, such as where a base block or a capital ends.
- Whether Base and Top are on, and their **fixed heights** in metres, are part of the curve: presets, saved curve files and the profile itself carry them, and picking a preset applies them in the same undo step. Columns, shafts, legs, posts, spindles, candlesticks, piers, buttresses and pilasters start with both on, each fixed at its share of the height the preset is drawn for (the Classic Column: 0.75 m base and capital). Props that scale as a whole, such as casks, drums, bottles, knives, finials, gables, merlons and brackets, start with both off. The picker still names a preset after its markers or heights are changed. New Columns start on the Classic Column with Base and Top on. Curves stored before this carry no heights and keep using the column's own Fixed base and top heights, so old scenes load unchanged.
- The Column card in the inspector has **Base** and **Top** buttons in a row under the Profile preview. They switch the same markers as the curve editor toggles (remembered places, one undo step each), light up while a marker is on, and update an open curve editor live. **Fixed Base Height** and **Fixed Top Height** edit the heights stored in the profile curve, one undo step each.
- The snap step starts at 0.01, the grid the presets are built on. The graph grid shows the snap step and every coarser decade (0.1 and 1), each fading in as its lines spread on screen, so lines are never closer than a few pixels. Both axes always use the same spacings, so every cell is a scaled copy of the unit square.
- **Undo** and **Redo** in the curve editor (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z) step the editor's normal undo history, where every curve edit is one step: a drag, a typed point, a tool or a preset. Undo in the main toolbar while the editor is open steps the same history and the editor follows, and in a collaboration room both are your per-user undo. The selection stays where it still exists.

## Saving and loading curves

- **Save** in the curve editor opens **Save Curve**: pick a location, type a name (default **Profile**) and save it as `<name>.aicurve`. With a project open the location is **Curves** (`AiWorldEd/Curves` in the project); in a collaboration room members always save to **Local** (their own); the room owner's **Remote** curves are read-only. An existing curve is only replaced after **Yes** in **Overwrite Curve?**. **Download** saves the file through the browser instead; without a project or room, Save downloads directly.
- **Load** opens **Load Curve** with the curves of each location; **From Disk…** picks a `.aicurve` file (older `.curve` and `.json` curve files load too).
- Curve tiles in the Project panel can be dragged onto a curve field.

## Prop and structure presets

The **Bottles & Containers**, **Medical**, **Tools & Hardware**, **Blades & Paddles**, **Antennas** and **Misc Props** categories, plus the piers and structures in the architecture categories, are everyday objects and structures. Presets store only the profile; the generator box sets the size, so each tooltip names a real-world box size, width by height. The column cannot hollow a shape, so pots, cans and bottles are solid.

## Preset categories

The column profile picker lists its presets in Title Case categories, in this order: **Columns**, **Column Shafts**, **Bases & Capitals**, **Piers & Buttresses**, **Walls, Beams & Trim**, **Brackets & Corbels**, **Towers & Monuments**, **Furniture Legs & Feet**, **Posts & Newels**, **Spindles & Balusters**, **Finials & Ornaments**, **Lighting**, **Bottles & Containers**, **Medical**, **Tools & Hardware**, **Blades & Paddles**, **Antennas** and **Misc Props**. Within a category presets go by family, then from simple to elaborate. One catalog (`curve_preset_catalog.ts`) owns the categories and their order by preset id; names and tooltips stay with each preset in sentence-case tooltips and Title Case names. Saved profiles store only the curve, and the picker recognises a preset by its shape, so renaming or regrouping presets never changes a saved scene.

## UVs and convex runs

**Cap Textures** (Texture group, off by default) reveals **Top Faces** and **Bottom Faces** texture pickers. A face counts as flat when its normal is within the cap-mapping limit of straight up or down (|normal.y| ≥ 0.999 in the generator frame), so it covers the column's caps, steps, ledges between differently sized or rotated segments, and the undersides of overhangs, for every section type, rotation, twist and merged run. Flat faces keep their world-aligned cap mapping and the texture slot's scale, offset and rotation; a picker left on Default keeps the main texture. Off, every face uses the main texture exactly as before. The three settings are only stored once used, and the chosen textures are remembered while the toggle is off.

U measures actual polygon perimeter rather than angular slices, giving consistent density across unequal rectangle and corner edges. Stretch closes once; Tile closes an integer number of times. V follows continuous profile arc length. Twisted triangles match their mapped corner coordinates exactly. Untwisted tapered quads retain the existing affine, exact-at-mid-height mapping.

Wrapping one image around a square and a circle of the same half-width necessarily changes physical density by 4/π in the circular limit: their perimeters differ. Perimeter mapping cannot remove this geometric ratio while preserving one closed wrap (or integer repeats). It removes unequal-side distortion, not this unavoidable shape-to-shape ratio.

The convex-run optimization already existed, and regression measurements before and after this work agree:

| Profile               | Sampled solid bands | Actual convex brushes, before → after |
| --------------------- | ------------------: | ------------------------------------: |
| Henry's Example.curve |                  22 |                               13 → 13 |
| Outward cubic Bezier  |                   8 |                                 1 → 1 |
| Straight shaft        |                   4 |                                 1 → 1 |
| Inward waist          |                   2 |                                 2 → 2 |

The outward Bezier also merges to one brush for Square, Polygon and a square with rounded corners. Untwisted merged and unmerged side UVs are identical. Type/parameter changes split runs; fixed markers split them where height remapping introduces a non-convex bend. A 90-degree twist produces 18 convex sub-band brushes rather than incorrectly merging the non-convex swept solid.

Henry's Weird.curve keeps three brushes with Sides 16. With middle Corner sides 1 → 4, the old shared sampling changed their vertex counts from 48/24/48 → 72/60/72. Independent sampling produces 32/24/32 → 32/60/32: the two Round brushes (including their UVs) are unchanged. Section boundaries remain capped horizontal brush junctions unless a segment blends.

## Persistence and verification

Section parameters and twist are stored in compact curve data and collaboration payloads; aspect belongs to the generator parameters. Authored generators/modifier volumes inside a solid-model hierarchy now survive that hierarchy snapshot in CSG order, with transforms and settings, instead of being silently dropped. Existing bevel-volume save loading is unchanged; no schema version or curve migration is introduced.

Tests cover all added profiles at non-unit aspect, independent section ring counts, convex twisted bands, UV seam/V continuity, hard-edge smoothing classes, curve editing, collaboration, scene save/load and bake. Browser acceptance covers marquee selection, all section choices, corner and numeric controls, native preset groups, textured profiles, and a baked single-brush wireframe-overlay view.

## Preset provenance

The grouped presets are original normalized profiles, not digitized plans, measured artifacts or historically exact reproductions; their tooltips give only general context. The two earlier softened furniture presets remain available. Generator bounds determine overall proportions. No source illustrations or plans are copied.

Terminology and form references checked:

- [Fine Woodworking, Elements of the Shaker Style](https://www.finewoodworking.com/membership/pdf/7527/011131079.pdf): simple square/turned tapers and swell tapers. The centered Shaker taper leg is **not** a traditional asymmetric inside-face taper.
- [Shackleton Fox, Turned Legs](https://www.shackletonfox.com/encyclopaedia/turned-legs/) and [Buffalo Architecture and History, Turning](https://buffaloah.com/f/glos/t/turn.html): cup-and-cover, ball-and-ring, baluster, bamboo, spool, steeple and trumpet vocabulary. The barley twist post combines a period-inspired silhouette with a stylized spiral, without attributing a particular historical object.
- [The Textbook of Architecture, Orders Compared](https://textbookofarchitecture.com/guide/orders-compared): Tuscan plain shafts and torus/plinth bases, Doric echinus, Ionic two-scotia bases and shaft diminution. Set height/diameter separately for order proportions. Doric and Ionic shafts deliberately omit flutes; no volutes, acanthus or complete capitals are implied.
- [UW–Madison Libraries, Arthur De Smet collection](https://search.library.wisc.edu/digital/ADeSmet): octagonal piers and molded bases in Decorated Gothic architecture. The pillar is simplified inspiration, not a surveyed pier.
- [Solomonic column](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomonic_column): twisting shafts and furniture use. The twisted presets use convex polygonal approximations, not exact carved ornament.
- Rotated stacks (all simplified, original proportions): [Turning Torso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turning_Torso) and [Calatrava, Turning Torso](https://calatrava.com/projects/turning-torso-malmoe.html): nine five-storey cube units turned to 90° at the top (**Turning Tower**). [Absolute World towers case study](https://misfitsarchitecture.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/5-case-study-absolute-world-towers-mississauga.pdf): floor plates turned 1° to 8° per floor, 209° in total, with wrap-around balconies (**Turning Floor Tower**, 16 floors turned up to 18° each). [Glossary of Architecture (Parker), buttresses](https://www.biblicalcyclopedia.com/B/buttress.html) and [Looking at Buildings glossary](http://www.lookingatbuildings.org.uk/glossary/glossary/D.html): Decorated Gothic angle buttresses and square pinnacles set diagonally (**Diagonal Pinnacle**). [Atlas Obscura, alphabet blocks](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/history-alphabet-blocks) and [The Strong, Alphabet Blocks](https://www.museumofplay.org/toys/alphabet-blocks/): Plat's 1594 letter dice and Locke's 1693 recommendation (**Block Stack**). The **Stone Cairn** is a generic stacked-stone marker.
- [Mark Baker, Rosewood Candlestick](https://woodworkersinstitute.com/how-to-make-a-rosewood-candlestick/) and [Popular Woodworking, Turned Table Lamp](https://www.popularwoodworking.live/article/turned-table-lamp/): bead/cove vocabulary, long coved stems and ogee lamp bases. All preset control points/proportions are original.
